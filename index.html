01 / A CONNECTING QUESTION

Different perspectives.
Shared challenges.

Environmental decisions rarely depend on science alone. People bring different knowledge, values, experiences and expectations. My research asks how those differences can be acknowledged and brought into decision-making—without assuming that disagreement will disappear.

01 · WHOSE?

Scientific, local and practitioner knowledge; values, perceptions, lived experiences and priorities.

02 · HOW?

Elicitation & integration

Participatory assessment, mixed methods, modelling, dialogue and multi-criteria approaches.

03 · WITH WHOM?

06 / ABOUT

Working between
disciplines, places
and people.

I am an environmental researcher working at the intersection of social–ecological systems, environmental decision science and collaborative governance.

My work spans participatory restoration assessment, water governance, climate adaptation and the integration of knowledge in contested decisions. Rather than treating participation as an end in itself, I am interested in what collaborative processes reveal, how they are designed and whether they meaningfully inform decisions.

I am an Assistant Professor at the Faculty of Forest Sciences and Nature Conservation, Universidad de Chile. My background includes a PhD in Arid Lands Resource Sciences from the University of Arizona and an MSc in integrated rural development and environmental management from the Mediterranean Agronomic Institute of Zaragoza.
